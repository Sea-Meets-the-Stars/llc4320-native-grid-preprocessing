## Definitions

### Standard

Our standard definition for the MLD the depth where the potential density is 0.03 kg m^-3 greater than the potential density at 10m below the surface.

It is calculated by the mixed_layer_depth function in src/dbof/preprocessing/calculated_fields_at_depth.py.

### Depth Integration Method

This method integrates the buoyancy frequency over the upper 300m and calculates the MLD as the depth-weighted value of the buoyancy frequency squared:


MLD = ∫_0^300m z N²(z) dz / ∫_0^300m N²(z) dz

where N²(z) is the buoyancy frequency squared at depth z.

**What I learned / gotchas**

- The density tile stores only `sigma0` (no Theta/Salt, no `drF`). Without a
  `drF`, `_get_vertical_spacing` falls back to building a layer thickness whose
  `k` coordinate carries *depth values*, which then fails to align with the
  integer-`k` N² field — yielding an all-NaN result. The fix (in both the test
  and notebook) is to supply a `drF` on the integer-`k` axis
  (`np.gradient(|Z|)`). Production `ds_merge` always carries `drF`, so this
  only bites on the bare density tiles. The reference level resolves to k=6
  (≈ 9.66 m), matching the existing `MLD_REFERENCE_DEPTH_M` note.
